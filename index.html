Ur · c. 2500 BCE · 40 seconds

A Ship from Meluhha

A trading boat from the Indus Valley — the land the Sumerians called Meluhha — arrives at the harbour of Ur with three of the goods it was known for.

Chapters

The three goods

Etched carnelian beads

The best-known Indus export. Beads like these were found in the Royal Cemetery of Ur (c. 2600–2500 BCE).

Square steatite seals

An Indus trademark, carved with animals such as the one-horned bull and a line of still-undeciphered script. Indus-style seals have turned up in Mesopotamia, including at Ur.

Ivory and fine timber

Mesopotamian texts list both among the goods that came from Meluhha, often through middlemen in Dilmun (Bahrain) and Magan (Oman).
